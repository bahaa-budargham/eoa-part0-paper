## Abstract

We introduce the Engineering of Alphabets (EOA), a research program built around a single observation that, if confirmed, should not exist according to current theories of representation. At the center of this program stands a recursive operator, denoted beta, which maps the spoken names of alphabet letters into numerical sequences. Preliminary observations suggest that under a specific bijective phonetic mapping for the 26 letters of the English alphabet, beta produces sequences that converge to a limiting constant ratio (LCR) of approximately 3.037. Under a different mapping, the LCR shifts to 2.689; under another, to 3.306. The sequences appear to collapse to a 16-element basis, and word vectors built from that basis seem to live on a very low-dimensional subspace, and hint at a recursive, tree-like generative structure underlying this collapse. These phenomena, if confirmed, are reproducible, encoding-dependent, and mathematically rigid. Yet none of them is currently understood.

This paper is Part 0 of the EOA Program. It contains no proofs and no conclusive experiments. Its purpose is to pose the questions that the subsequent five papers will address, and to situate those questions within the broader scientific landscape where symbolic dynamics, phonetic encoding theory, low-rank representation learning, and deterministic machine learning currently converge.
